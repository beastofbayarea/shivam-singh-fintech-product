## Evidence-grounded premise

NIST's GenAI Profile frames lifecycle risks for generative systems. NIST Zero Trust Architecture requires least-privilege, per-request access decisions rather than implicit trust. These sources support financial-services agents that operate within explicit identities, permissions, evidence requirements, approval thresholds, and recoverable workflows.

## Case approach

- Define allowed tasks, prohibited actions, data boundaries, and responsible owners by workflow.
- Assign identities and least-privilege permissions to users, agents, tools, and services.
- Require evidence, policy checks, and human approval before consequential actions.
- Test injection, tool misuse, data leakage, incorrect advice, rollback, and audit reconstruction.

## Evidence-based success measures

Use policy coverage, unauthorized-action rate, evidence completeness, approval accuracy, escalation quality, recovery, and audit traceability. These are proposed measures, not historical results.
